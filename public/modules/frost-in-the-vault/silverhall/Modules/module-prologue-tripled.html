Prologue: Cracks in the Coin

A Kingdom on the Edge

Brevoy stands as a precarious chimera — part frost, part flame. Forged in the uneasy union of Issia’s northern glaciers and Rostland’s southern plains, its spine is political compromise bound with gold-threaded lies. House Surtova, the so-called stewards of the realm, rule from their icy seat in New Stetven, but their legitimacy was always a matter of convenience, not conviction. They were to hold the crown only so long as no one better came along.

Yet decades passed. No champions rose. The crown, meant to be temporary, calcified around Surtovan brows.

Now, the cracks show.

Across the southern roads, conscripted laborers vanish into road crews and border garrisons with no war declared. Grain is weighed before it’s taxed, then weighed again. Coin is no longer accepted unless stamped fresh within the past year. Word travels that mercenaries now wear noble colors, their armor enameled in Surtova’s black and silver.

Nobles whisper behind closed doors — of disappearances and substitutions, of allies “replaced” with more compliant cousins. House Garess refuses trade with the north. House Medvyed patrols its own borders like a sovereign state. Even the notoriously aloof House Lebeda has begun quiet audits of its coin reserves.

And yet the peasants say little. The merchants bow. The temples pray. For the coins still clink, and coins are what hold kingdoms together.

Until now.

The Hollow Chill

Beneath the stone-thick frost of Port Ice, where only the desperate and well-funded dare mine the glacial crust, there lies a forge not built by dwarves or men. It hums beneath the ice like a frozen heart, warmed by neither flame nor faith.

There, something was reborn.

Not made. Not summoned. Born — in the ancient tongue of Issia, it is named ʐɨr'vokk'san, "The Stillness That Consumes."

To modern tongues, it is called the Hollow Chill.

It is neither curse nor poison. It is a subtle perversion of alchemical truth — an agent injected during the refining of precious metals. Harmless in its infancy, it bonds with copper, silver, gold, even platinum, creating coins indistinguishable from the genuine. For months — or even years — they function normally.

Until they don’t.

Heat, pressure, the passage of time — any will awaken the reaction. The metal begins to flake. Then crumble. Then disintegrate entirely into brilliant, useless dust.

There are no marks. No glyphs. No magical auras.

And by the time anyone notices, the metal has changed hands a hundred times. Wealth has moved. Debts have vanished. Alliances paid for in dust disintegrate with equal silence.

Surtova, of course, denies all. Their emissaries speak of counterfeiters and foreign agitators. But their caravans pass uninspected. Their ships leave port faster. Their vaults remain heavy, their gifts heavier still. Coins without weight. Bribes without record.

Some claim the Hollow Chill is a weapon. Others believe it’s a test. But only the desperate dare ask who invented it — and who will be the next to fall to it.

House Orlovsky’s Quiet Desperation

Beyond the Golushkin Mountains, in Grayhaven, a different silence festers. It is the hush of an anxious court — and a ledger that cannot be balanced.

House Orlovsky, the last true bastion of old Rostland pride, has begun to flinch. Their gold, once the envy of Brevoy, no longer gleams as brightly. Coin hoards in subterranean vaults — sealed by oath and steel — now show signs of disintegration. A single shipment of platinum intended for foreign mercenaries dissolved in full view of Orlovsky’s generals.

Their treasurers can no longer guarantee the weight of their silver. Nor the loyalty of those they pay with it.

Yet they cannot speak openly. To accuse House Surtova without proof is to call down war.

Instead, they send whispers south. To Silverhall. To House Lebeda — wealthy, distant, and rumored to have uncovered the first crack in the conspiracy.

Silverhall: The First Fracture

It begins not with battle — but balance sheets.

In the marbled silence of a noble counting hall, beneath the quiet gaze of Abadar’s golden seal, a single coin is removed from a sealed box. It was stamped thirty years ago. It has never left the vault.

The high priest blesses it. The scribe notes its origin.

And then it crumbles in the hand of a junior alchemist — a gnome named Ilexi Tinctwhistle.

At first, she is blamed. Then, she is believed. With trembling hands, she mixes reagents into a fluid that glows green when near infected metal. It isn’t perfect. It fades over time. But it reveals enough.

Enough to terrify.

House Lebeda locks down its vaults. Cancels its pending payments. Calls back three of its most senior coinmasters.

And sends six letters.

No banners. No public edicts. Just green wax, and the authority of desperation.

The Six

The letters do not go to generals or knights. They go to outcasts. To specialists. To ghosts the crown has tried to forget.

  • Velra Wynne — A tiefling inquisitor of Abadar, once cast out for exposing a noble's fraud and surviving the political fallout.
  • Karzak Deepstem — A dwarven cave druid who lost more than comrades in a mine collapse — and still speaks to the stone like it listens.
  • Lazlo Oerlen — A half-elf magus whose frost spells fracture oddly near tainted metal — and who fled Port Ice after a spell misfired.
  • Ilexi Tinctwhistle — The alchemist herself, now marked by those who wish her reagent unmade.
  • Sava of Zmeyka — River-runner, smuggler, blade-for-hire. She’s kept one foot on each side of the Gray River her entire life — and betrayed both.
  • Serune Quen — A dhampir armiger once sworn to House Surtova. She left when her orders grew colder than her blood. Now she wears black steel and no crest.

They are summoned to Silverhall. They are to act without names, without laws, and without fail.

They are not heroes. They are not chosen. They are tools in a vault gone cold.

And the weight of Brevoy now rests on what they find — or fail to stop.
