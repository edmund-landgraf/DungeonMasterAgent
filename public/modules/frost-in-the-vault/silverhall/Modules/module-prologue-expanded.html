Frost in the Vaults: Silverhall – Prologue

Prologue: Cracks in the Coin

A Kingdom on the Edge

Brevoy is a nation balanced between the brittle bones of two older kingdoms — Issia and Rostland — stitched together by ambition and crowned in compromise. House Surtova, ruling from New Stetven, was never meant to be a permanent solution. They were a placeholder, a patch across the wound of civil war. But now, the patch is tightening.

The people whisper of conscripted labor, new taxes on southern grain, and mercenaries in noble colors. The nobles whisper of assassins, of sudden arrests, and of “replacements.” It is said that Port Ice has become quiet — too quiet — and that Grayhaven no longer answers every courier.

The noble houses play their game. The coins do not complain. Until now.

The Hollow Chill

In an unmarked cavern beneath the frost-forges of Port Ice, a compound was born. Not made, not summoned — born. It is called the Hollow Chill, though that is only the name given by gnomes and frightened chemists. Its true name is rendered in ancient Issian glyphs: ʐɨr'vokk'san, the Stillness That Consumes.

Injected during the forging process, this compound lies dormant in gold, silver, copper, and platinum — invisible to priest, wizard, or merchant. Its magic awakens only when exposed to heat, time, or commerce. Then the metal collapses into gleaming dust, inert and useless. Trade halts. Bribes become void. Armies go unpaid.

House Surtova claims no knowledge. But their ships arrive more quickly. Their debts vanish more cleanly. Their agents travel with forged permits and generous coin — too generous, if one weighs it closely.

House Orlovsky’s Quiet Desperation

Far to the west, behind the mountains, House Orlovsky watches its silver fade. In Grayhaven, ledgers go red despite full coffers. Merchants report that wagons return with their own coin — but thinner, lighter, dust-tinged. The House dare not accuse the crown. Not openly. But they look south, to House Lebeda, and ask for help.

For now, this help comes not in banners or treaties, but in secrets. Lebeda sends word through hidden channels. “We have found one coin. It broke beneath a shrine of Abadar.”

Silverhall: The First Fracture

Silverhall has always claimed wealth, but rarely truth. Its noble vaults are ringed with law runes, its mints are kept under divine audit, and its forges — once cooled — have remained silent since the last purge. Until now. A vault door groans open after decades untouched. Within it: a sealed coffer of gold, still marked with the sigil of Abadar.

The coin crumbles. Not rust. Not spellwork. Something colder. Something meant to break it.

Alchemist Ilexi Tinctwhistle is the first to identify the contaminant. She creates a reagent that glows green in its presence — and fades when near pure metal. It is not perfect. But it is a beginning. House Lebeda convenes quietly with select allies. They send six letters. Sealed in green wax.

The Six

The letters do not go to knights. They do not go to generals. They go to people no one will miss — and everyone should fear.

  • Velra Wynne – A tiefling inquisitor of Abadar, disgraced for refusing a noble’s bribe — and surviving the fallout.
  • Karzak Deepstem – A cave druid who speaks to stone and remembers every death the mountain has buried.
  • Lazlo Oerlen – A half-elf magus whose frost-wrought spells act strangely near infected silver. He does not know why.
  • Ilexi Tinctwhistle – A gnome chemist whose reagent can identify the Hollow Chill. She has already been warned to stay quiet.
  • Sava of Zmeyka – A sharp-eyed rogue from the Gray River ports. Her blades are faster than her tongue — and her tongue is fast indeed.
  • Serune Quen – A dhampir armiger, disavowed by Surtova for “excessive restraint.” She carries her old weapons — and a death sentence.

They are to investigate. Quietly. Firmly. Without name or banner. What they find will shape the fate of Brevoy — or break it into coins and dust.
