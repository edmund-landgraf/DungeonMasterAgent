The Hollow Chill

A fusion of known metallurgy and unnatural alchemical interference.

Metallic Bonding and Real-World Metallurgy

In traditional smelting and metal refining, base ore is heated to high temperatures to separate impurities. During this process, the atomic lattice of metals like gold, silver, copper, or platinum reforms as the material cools. Despite the intense heat, the fundamental metallic bonding remains intact. These metallic bonds are stabilized by a "sea" of delocalized electrons, which gives metals their conductivity, malleability, and structural cohesion.

Even when reheated and remelted, the metal’s elemental structure does not change — it simply transitions through physical states. Once cooled, it resumes a stable crystalline lattice governed by metallic bonds. That is what makes the Hollow Chill particularly insidious: it does not interfere with elemental identity, but replaces the bonding framework during the cooling phase.

The Coldheart Injector Process

The Hollow Chill is made possible through a forbidden device known as the Coldheart Injector. This machine is surgically installed inside a smelter or crucible housing. It operates during the brief window as molten metal begins to cool — precisely the phase where atoms rearrange into a solid lattice.

The injector releases a measured brine filament — derived from the liquified ichor of the ancient race known as the Zirvokksan — into the molten stream. This ichor is cryo-alchemically reactive, bonding not to the elemental gold or silver, but to the spatial lattice structure forming during cooling. The result is an artificially stabilized “frost-bond” layer that mimics normal metal — but only while kept cold and unagitated.

Alchemical Explanation

The cryo-ichor brine forms a temporary sublattice around the delocalized electron sea of the metal. In ideal conditions (low temperature, low motion), the structure behaves like stable metal. However, when exposed to heat, vibration, or thaumaturgical interference, the sublattice destabilizes — causing molecular collapse.

Unlike typical decay or oxidation, the Hollow Chill does not tarnish or rot the metal — it causes complete atomic separation. The final result is dust indistinguishable from processed metal shavings, but without mass, conductivity, or weight.
